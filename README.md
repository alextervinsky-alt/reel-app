# Reel

A personal Mac app that scans film drives, recognises each film from its file name, and shows it as a good-looking library to help decide what to watch. Bonus material in a film's folder (interviews, trailers, an `Extras` folder, subtitles, notes) is kept with the film instead of showing up as films of its own. Reel never changes, moves or writes film files and never writes to film drives: scanning only reads folder listings (names, sizes, dates), and files are only opened when you press Play or open an extra.

Everything that comes from the web refreshes on its own while Reel is open (checked at launch and every six hours, each item only when due): film info and ratings monthly, award lists weekly, collecting sets every two weeks, the IMDb ratings copy and list posters monthly, Explore lists every six hours.

**All Films** opens in a random order, different each time you come back to it (or by title, year, rating, date added or running time), with a search field beside its title (⌘F); the banner at the top opens its film. When Reel opens it reads the library in the background and scans every connected drive; drives connected later are scanned as they appear. Films found since a drive's first scan show under **New Arrivals** for 14 days, and a wishlist film that turns up in the library leaves the wishlist on its own (tagged "From your wishlist" there). A drive marked as **Backup** is compared with the main drive by film and file size, and **Not on Backup** lists what's missing or only half copied. **Recommended** offers five films per launch, with mood chips (from the moods All Films already uses, plus Short). **Year in Film** sums up what you watched each year (films, hours, month by month, highlights, then in numbers: the average film, your average stars, new releases, your busiest month and film night, how many countries your films were made in and languages spoken in them; genres, directors you came back to, languages and the faces you saw most); films you saw elsewhere (the cinema, streaming) can be dated, rated and counted in it or left out with one switch. A film marked seen elsewhere is dated the month after it came out (or now, for a film still in cinemas), which you can change; films marked before Reel did this can be dated by their release in one click. A wrongly grouped file can be fixed with a right-click ("This Is an Extra Of…", "This Is a Film"); the fix is kept in Your Notes and applied on every scan.

**Spoiler-safe** (on by default): until a film is marked watched, review quotes (each shown once) and **Behind the Film** keep the story, twists and ending hidden, each with "Show anyway". Behind the Film starts with three things to know and the film at a glance, then tells its Wikipedia article as the film's life from idea to legacy: numbered chapters in the order it lived them (the idea, casting, the shoot, design and effects, music, release, how it was received, awards, legacy), whatever order the article uses. Each opens on its best lines, someone's own words set apart and a fact or two, and opens in place to read the whole of it. *Before you watch* has the making of, context and reception; *After you watch* (the story, its themes, every paragraph that gives something away) stays closed until the film is watched. **Where It Was Filmed** lists every filming place with what it is (from Wikidata) and the article's own sentences about the locations, studios and stages. Links to Roger Ebert, Criterion, the BFI, Senses of Cinema, Letterboxd and IMDb follow. Under the title, badges show the lists a film is on (Palme d'Or, Sight & Sound, Criterion, IMDb Top 250). **Tonight** is a shortlist for the evening, compared side by side in aligned rows (year and genres, director and leads, ratings, verdict, why it's offered, premise, end time, moods, subtitles and quality, the lists it's on), cleared at 6 in the morning; a row none of the films has anything for is left out. Recommended learns from your shared star ratings and says why each film is there in one short line (a film you loved by the same director, the watchlist, new on a drive, a list it's on, or the best in a mood); "Not tonight" keeps a film out for two weeks. Marking a film watched asks "How was it?". Films open in IINA, VLC or the default app; a film you have in two versions asks which to play.

**Cinema mode** (⇧⌘C, the TV button in the sidebar, the View menu or Settings) turns Reel into a 10-foot view for the TV: full screen and edge to edge (the window buttons slide in when the pointer reaches the top), with big targets meant for a phone used as a mouse and a Back button always at the top. It keeps only what helps decide, in text of 22 points or more. Three tabs: **For You** (at most 15 rows in a fixed order: Tonight, Recommended, New Arrivals, Like the Films You Loved, Watchlist, up to six genres, up to three languages with enough unwatched films, Under 100 Minutes, Acclaimed and Watch Again; within each row films of about the same rating are shuffled once per evening), **Tonight** (the shortlist in aligned columns with **Decide for Us**) and **All Films** (one row of five filters, sort, mood, length, language and unwatched, each opening a large panel, and a search field; Random is the default sort). A poster at rest shows its title and year · running time; under the pointer or the arrow keys its ratings take the second line, and the poster shows when it would end, a moon for Tonight and a trailer bar. Return opens a film (and plays it on its page), Space plays the trailer, T adds to or takes off Tonight, Esc goes back. A film's page fits one screen and says HDR or SDR beside its year, with Reviews (the critics' consensus, what people like and don't like, awards), From the Same People and More Like This below. Turning Cinema mode off returns the window and the library exactly as they were.

Films open full screen in IINA or VLC (Settings › Watching › Play full screen, always in Cinema mode; VLC is asked through AppleScript, which macOS confirms once, and with VLC as the player it's started out of sight when a film page, Recommended or Cinema mode opens, so Play doesn't wait for it to load; Reel 1.7.1 switched a VLC setting to IINA once, for HDR on the TV), and coming back to Reel after watching long enough asks "Did you finish it?", then "How was it?". Under a film's title a tag says whether it's HDR (Dolby Vision, HDR10+, HDR10, HLG) or SDR, read from the file name, so the TV can be set before Play. A film's page has five tabs: Overview, Reviews, Behind the Film, Camera and Extras (file details are in the "…" menu). **Camera** is for the cinematographer. It reads the film's Wikipedia article, the interviews and craft articles it cites (American Cinematographer, British Cinematographer, Kodak, ARRI, No Film School, IndieWire's craft pieces…), American Cinematographer's own articles about the film and the cinematographer's Wikipedia article, then shows: who shot it and who they are, frames from the film, the look in brief, a technical specification sheet (camera, lenses, film and format, aspect ratio, frame rate, colour, lighting, grip and movement, filters, lab and finish; formats only when the sentence is about the shooting, not the release), the filmmakers' own words, why it looks this way, camera and movement, lighting, and colour and grade (three notes each, the rest a click away), each with where it was read and the gear in bold (never from what critics or awards said, or from the music, release and reception sections, and nothing about the story before the film is watched). Then the camera, lighting and colour crew from TMDB, the interviews to read in full, and links to IMDb's technical specifications, ShotOnWhat and American Cinematographer. Overview has **From the Same People** (director, writer, cinematographer, composer, then the two leads, up to 12 films) and **More Like This**, which ranks films in your library by what viewers of the film went on to like (TMDB recommendations), rare shared themes, tone and genre, and keeps family films and documentaries apart from adult films, and gentle films (animated, family, feel-good) apart from violent or bleak ones however many themes they share. **Trailers** are the film's own trailers and teasers on YouTube, in English, with no language or in the film's language, HD uploads first and played at 1080p when YouTube has it, the studio's uploads first and others' (an archive's) only as a fallback; TMDB's label isn't trusted alone, so a video named a TV spot, clip, featurette, announcement, reaction or fan edit is never played (the teaser for unwatched films while spoiler-safe is on, only one named a teaser) and play inside Reel in YouTube's privacy-enhanced player over the film's backdrop, nothing kept on disk, with Reel's own controls: click the timeline to jump anywhere (or drag), click the picture or press Space to pause, ← and → for ten seconds back or on. Full Screen (or F) fills the screen with the player, and a trailer started from a preview plays in the window's own player. Up to five are tried in turn; when one ends, the film's backdrop and Play Again cover YouTube's suggestions; when none plays the button disappears, and only the library offers "Watch on YouTube". Cinema mode never opens a browser, and if three films in a row fail it hides trailers until Reel is opened again. In the library, Space on the poster under the pointer (or right-click › Quick Look) shows a quick preview (a click outside it or Esc closes it, as with a preview of a film you don't own), and the moon on a poster under the pointer adds it to Tonight; All Films filters by mood chips, length and language. Settings › Performance lists how long opening, scanning and updating took; Settings scrolls at a height that fits a laptop.

**For You** (under Discover) is fifteen films you don't have and haven't seen that people who loved your favourites (4 or 5 stars, or a heart) went on to love, kept only when they're truly alike (the same kind of film, from about the same time, sharing people, themes or tone), each saying which of your films it follows, with its trailer, ratings and synopsis; fifteen others each time Reel opens (and New Picks draws again). **Explore** has a search for any film (those in your library first), what's in cinemas, trending and new at home, three shelves that change each time Reel opens (Because You Loved one of your films, a country's cinema, a decade or the hidden gems), and the best of any year from the source you choose (TMDB or IMDb rating, most popular, award winners, hidden gems), the source and the year each picked in one click from a short list. Films you've seen are left out, and rows that rank by quality are reordered each launch within films of about the same rating. Mood chips narrow every row to one mood and add that mood's best-loved films from any time. The Lists hub starts with a search for lists by any words (a theme like heist or lighthouse, a series, a person, a country's cinema or a decade, and Wikipedia's "List of … films"), then **Start Here**: the films the critics' polls, festivals and rankings agree on that you haven't seen, those already on your drives and those worth finding, ranked by how many (and which) lists they're on, how they fit your ratings and how they're rated, each saying why (a director whose film you loved, or its lists). Then IMDb's Top 100 for any year and Top 250 (from IMDb's free non-commercial ratings files), award winners from Wikidata (Oscars for Best Picture, Director, Cinematography, International and Animated Film, Cannes, Venice, Berlin, Locarno, BAFTA, César, European Film Awards), Sight & Sound and the Criterion Collection, each showing what you own and have seen, sortable by Best for You, and saying which other lists a film is on; and "Complete the Set" for directors, cinematographers and franchises. Marking a film seen asks when and how it was.

## Code

- `Sources/ReelCore`: logic with no UI: file-name reader, TMDB client, matcher, drive scanner (with film/extras grouping in `Extras.swift` and hand fixes in `GroupingFixes.swift`), new arrivals, wishlist cleanup and the Backup check (`Arrivals.swift`), recommendations (`Recommender.swift`), Year in Film, IMDb ratings files, Wikidata lists, film sets, library, storage
- `Sources/Reel/Model`: app state (`AppModel`, split by topic into `AppModel+….swift`), notes observed per film (`PersonalStore`), drive locating by volume ID, image cache
- `Sources/Reel/Views`: sidebar, grid, film page, match fixer, welcome, settings; `Views/Cinema` is Cinema mode, a separate view tree, so switching never disturbs the desk views
- `Sources/Reel/Diagnostics/ScreenRenderer.swift`: draws Cinema mode's screens to PNG at laptop, 1080p and 4K sizes (CI only, from a temporary sample library)
- `Tests/ReelCoreTests`: run on every push; `LiveTMDBTests` matches the sample films against the real TMDB
- `scripts/make-app.sh`: builds `Reel.app` (Apple Silicon, macOS 15+), signs with a certificate only if the `SIGNING_CERT_P12` secret is ever added

## Where Reel keeps things

Everything is in `~/Documents/Reel/`:

| | |
|---|---|
| `Your Notes.json` | watched, favorites, watchlist, ratings, notes, wishlist, match and grouping fixes (irreplaceable; set aside, never overwritten, if unreadable) |
| `Settings.json` | TMDB token, OMDb key, drives and which one is the Backup (mode 600) |
| `Library.json` | film info per file: a pure cache, rebuilt by a rescan |
| `Safety Copies/` | one copy of Your Notes per day, last 30 days |
| `Image Cache.nosync/` | posters and photos: 400 MB cap (least recently used removed first), unused images removed after a week, excluded from iCloud and Time Machine |
| `List Data.nosync/` | IMDb ratings copy, award lists, list posters and sets (downloaded again if deleted) |

Writes are atomic, batched (notes 0.5 s, library 2 s) and run on a background queue; everything is flushed on quit. Network requests use no disk cache and no cookies. Window state restoration is off. Nothing is ever written to film drives.

## Builds

GitHub Actions builds on every push with `[build]` in the commit message, or by hand. Download `Reel-mac` from the run; the screens (Cinema mode at the laptop's size, 1080p and 4K, plus a few library views) are pushed to the `screens` branch.

Builds carry no keys. The TMDB token and the OMDb key (for IMDb, Rotten Tomatoes and Metacritic ratings, free from omdbapi.com) are pasted once in Reel and kept only in `Settings.json`; CI reads its own copies from the repository's secrets (`TMDB_TOKEN`, `OMDB_KEY`) for the live tests and the screens (TMDB only), and never prints them. The file names in the tests are made up.
